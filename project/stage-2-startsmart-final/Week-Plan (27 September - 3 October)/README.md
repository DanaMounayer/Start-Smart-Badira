## The seven papers behind the questions

| | Paper | The finding that drives a question |
|---|---|---|
| **P1** | Krishnamurti et al. (2021), *JAMA Network Open* | Of women with a documented aspirin recommendation in their chart, only **63%** knew about it |
| **P2** | Shanmugalingam et al. (2020), *PLoS ONE* | **44%** of women prescribed aspirin were less than 90% adherent, measured objectively |
| **P3** | Preeclampsia in Saudi Arabia — systematic review & meta-analysis (2025) | **4.80%** pooled prevalence; **27%** awareness |
| **P4** | *Cureus* (2023) — awareness among Saudi women | Knowledge of warning signs is limited |
| **P5** | Siddiqui (2016), *Rawal Medical Journal* | Mean booking **12.2 weeks**; **36%** book late — the screening window is closing as women arrive |
| **P6** | *Macedonian Medical Review* (2019) | **22%** of women attending check-ups had no blood pressure measured |
| **P7** | *npj Digital Medicine* (2022), Mount Sinai | Pregnancy **trajectories** predict better than single readings |

> **P5 is the hinge.** Combined first-trimester screening runs at 11–14 weeks. If more than a third
> of women arrive after that, the window is the problem — not the model.
